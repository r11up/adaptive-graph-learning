FINDING 14 — PLAN A (LEARNED PROJECTION): RAISES CLASSICAL, LOWERS QUANTUM
===========================================================================
Status: measured, all four cohorts, 62 folds, 4,456 subjects
Date:   2026-08-28
Raw:    results/superseded-prefix/ABIDE_hybrid/, results/superseded-prefix/planA/{ADHD-200,REST-meta-MDD,UCLA-CNP}/


THE IDEA
--------
FINDING 11 located the constraint on the quantum arm as the feature budget
rather than the model: at eight features quantum and classical tie, while the
same classical model given all 2000 reaches 0.649. The register caps how much
reaches the circuit.

Plan A replaces fixed t-test selection with a linear projection R^2000 -> R^8
learned jointly with the circuit, so the quantum stage receives an optimised
summary of every connection instead of eight raw ones — more information, no
extra qubits.

Every quantum head was given a classical twin with an identical projection,
identical bottleneck width and identical training, plus a linear-head control
to measure how much of any gain came from the projection alone.

PLAN A FAILS, AND IT FAILS SIGNIFICANTLY
----------------------------------------
Against fixed selection (FINDING 13):

  quantum:   QCNN 0.610 -> H-QCNN 0.549      worse
             VQC  0.575 -> H-VQC  0.522      worse
  classical: MLP  0.600 -> H-MLP  0.642      better
             CNN  0.589 -> H-CNN  0.632      better

The learned projection helps the classical arm and hurts the quantum arm, and
all four paired tests reject at p < 0.05 — the first comparisons in this study
where classical beats quantum with real statistical weight.

H-MLP at 0.642 accuracy and 0.709 AUC is the best result any neural model has
reached on ABIDE here, second only to the 2000-feature SVM at 0.649/0.738.


THE MECHANISM
-------------
H-Linear is the diagnostic. A single linear layer on the learned projection
reaches 0.624 accuracy and 0.704 AUC — within 0.02 of the best classical head.
The projection is doing nearly all the work.

The quantum head then makes it worse. A bounded Pauli-Z expectation is a poorer
classifier on top of a good 8-dimensional projection than a plain linear layer
is. Whatever the circuit adds does not compensate for compressing a well-chosen
representation through a bounded scalar read-out.


TWO FAULTS FIXED ALONG THE WAY, BOTH BY MEASUREMENT
---------------------------------------------------
1. A randomly initialised projection left the quantum head at 0.528 against
   0.610 for the same circuit on fixed features. Optimising 2000 projection
   parameters through a bounded expectation value does not work. Warm-starting
   at the fixed-selection solution means the projection can only depart from
   that baseline if departing helps.

2. A plain sigmoid squashed angle spread, placing standardised inputs in its
   flat tails. This is the failure FINDING 01 measured, where restoring angle
   spread was worth 0.62 to 0.97 AUC. Normalising per dimension before bounding
   fixed it.

Together these took the hybrid QCNN from 0.528 to 0.582 on a 300-subject
subset, where the gaps looked non-significant. THAT DID NOT SURVIVE THE FULL
COHORT: on 20 folds the same comparison is -0.078 at p = 0.0079. A subset of
six folds was not enough to judge, and the intermediate reading was wrong.


WHAT IT IS WORTH
----------------
The negative result is informative. It shows the feature-budget diagnosis was
right about where the headroom is — the classical arm gained 0.042 accuracy
from exactly the intervention the diagnosis predicted — while showing the
quantum head cannot exploit that headroom, because its bounded read-out is the
weaker classifier once the representation is good.

It also produced the study's best neural result on ABIDE (H-MLP, 0.642/0.709),
which is worth reporting on its own terms.

=== ADDENDUM 2026-08-28: EXTENDED TO ALL FOUR COHORTS ===

It replicates on the other well-powered cohort. REST-meta-MDD, 25 folds:

  H-QCNN vs H-CNN     -0.047  5/25  p = 0.0244 *
  H-VQC  vs H-MLP     -0.033  7/25  p = 0.0553
  H-QCNN vs H-Linear  -0.069  8/25  p = 0.0025 **
  H-VQC  vs H-Linear  -0.034  7/25  p = 0.0255 *

Seven of eight tests across ABIDE-I and REST-meta-MDD reject, all against the
quantum arm; the eighth is at p = 0.055.

Four-cohort accuracy:
  cohort           H-QCNN  H-VQC  H-MLP  H-CNN  H-Linear
  ABIDE-I           0.549  0.522  0.642  0.632  0.624
  ADHD-200          0.554  0.494  0.540  0.498  0.495
  REST-meta-MDD     0.542  0.559  0.594  0.583  0.600
  UCLA-CNP          0.496  0.527  0.589  0.557  0.580

Across 40 pairwise tests: 10 significant against 2.0 expected by chance (5x the
chance rate), and ONE SURVIVES BONFERRONI (p = 0.0006 vs threshold 0.00125).
Losing arm in all ten: H-QCNN x6, H-VQC x4 -- never a classical arm.

This is the strongest statistical result in the study. Contrast FINDING 17,
where six nominally significant results all shared one broken comparator; here
the effect survives every comparator including H-Linear, on both cohorts with
adequate folds. ADHD-200 (7 folds) and UCLA-CNP (10) return nothing
significant, with point estimates negative in six of eight -- underpowered, not
contradictory.

Note ADHD-200 is the one cohort where H-QCNN is nominally best (0.554). It is
also the smallest fold count in the study and nothing there is significant.
