FINDING 21 — NO LOW-DATA CROSSOVER: REGULARISATION WITHOUT ADVANTAGE
=====================================================================
Status: measured, controlled subsampling, ABIDE-I (4 sizes) + REST-meta-MDD (3)
Date:   2026-08-29
Code:   scripts/run_lowdata.py
Raw:    results/superseded-prefix/lowdata/{abide,mdd}/lowdata_results.json


THE PREDICTION BEING TESTED
---------------------------
FINDING 18 measured a quantum advantage in overfitting resistance whose margin
grew as cohorts shrank, and at the smallest cohort (UCLA-CNP, n=226) the
quantum arms led on BOTH gap and accuracy. That implied a crossover: below some
training size, quantum should win on accuracy too.

The test holds leave-site-out folds fixed and subsamples only the TRAINING
pool, three draws per fold per size. Test sets are unchanged, so the comparison
is clean.

Interpretation was fixed in advance: a crossover on both cohorts is a real
low-data effect; on one cohort only it is noise; none falsifies the FINDING 18
extrapolation.

THE TWO HALVES COME APART
-------------------------
CONFIRMED: quantum has the smaller overfitting gap at EVERY size on BOTH
cohorts, 1.5x to 2.2x, and the margin widens as n falls. This reproduces
FINDING 18 by controlled subsampling rather than by comparing cohorts of
different size -- the stronger form of the evidence.

FALSIFIED: quantum is best on accuracy at NONE of the seven sizes, and is
significantly worse than an 18-parameter linear model at two of them. There is
no crossover and no trend toward one; the accuracy gap narrows as n GROWS,
the opposite of what a low-data quantum advantage predicts.


WHAT TO CONCLUDE
----------------
Structural regularisation from a bounded read-out is real, quantum-specific,
and strongest exactly where classical models struggle most. It is still not an
advantage, because the classical models that overfit most (C-MLP) are not the
ones worth beating. A linear classifier is better regularised AND more accurate
at every size tested.

This is a sharper result than either outcome alone would have been. "Quantum
wins in the low-data regime" would have been the more attractive headline and
is not what the data says; "nothing happens" is also not what the data says.
The precise statement is a dissociation between a real mechanism and an absent
benefit.


PROCEDURAL NOTE
---------------
The MDD run was stopped after n=400 of a planned {100,200,400,800,1600}. This
was sound rather than merely convenient: the hypothesis concerned SMALL n, the
small-n end was complete on both cohorts and uniformly negative, and the
remaining points were the large-n end where FINDING 17 already showed a tie.
The untested sizes could not have rescued the hypothesis. Three sizes on MDD
and four on ABIDE-I are what is reported.
