WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SUPPORT
-----------------------------------
SUPPORTED: the corrected quantum arm is competitive, not deficient. On ABIDE-I
it leads on AUC, MCC and balanced accuracy against matched comparators, and it
resists overfitting better than any neural comparator on every cohort.

NOT SUPPORTED: a general quantum advantage. Quantum is ahead in fewer than half
of all comparisons (113/284). Nothing survives Bonferroni correction at the
scale of the sweep. The accuracy advantage never reaches significance on a
non-degenerate comparator. And the pattern is cohort-specific: almost every
clean win is on ABIDE-I, the cohort with the most extractable signal
(headroom +0.104 against +0.036 for MDD and +0.010 for ADHD).

THE HONEST ONE-LINE SUMMARY: quantum ranks as well or better than matched
classical models on the cohort with real signal, generalises with less
overfitting everywhere, and converts neither into a significant accuracy
advantage anywhere.


A NOTE ON METRIC CHOICE
-----------------------
That quantum leads far more often on AUC (34/68) than on accuracy (24/72) is
itself informative. AUC measures ranking; accuracy measures ranking plus
threshold placement. A model whose scores rank well but whose decision
threshold is poorly calibrated will look worse on accuracy than it deserves.
The QCNN reads out a bounded Pauli-Z expectation mapped to logits by a
two-parameter affine layer, which is a weak calibration mechanism. This
suggests threshold calibration as a cheap and untested improvement, and it is
the most promising remaining direction the results point to.
