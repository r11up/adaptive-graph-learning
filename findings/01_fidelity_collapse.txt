FINDING 01 — QUANTUM FIDELITY COLLAPSES WITH REGISTER WIDTH
===========================================================
Status: measured, reproducible, contradicts the source manuscript
Date:   2026-08-15
Code:   src/qagta/quantum/fmri_encoder.py, src/qagta/quantum/fidelity.py
Data:   ABIDE I, 1035 subjects, 20 sites, CC200 / C-PAC filt_noglobal


CLAIM
-----
Quantum state fidelity |<psi_i|psi_j>|^2 between regional embeddings loses all
discriminative power as the qubit register widens. At 16 qubits — the width the
manuscript specifies — no pair of brain regions has fidelity above 0.01, so the
metric cannot order neighbours and any topology derived from it is noise.


MEASUREMENT
-----------
Real ABIDE subjects, 200 CC200 regions each, ring-entangled encoder,
fidelity computed over all 19,900 region pairs:

  qubits   Hilbert dim   mean fidelity   max      pairs > 0.01
  ------   -----------   -------------   ------   ------------
  4        16            0.0624          0.970    47.5%
  8        256           0.0039          0.662     7.4%
  12       4,096         0.0003          0.144      0.6%
  16       65,536        0.0000          0.023      0.0%

Downstream consequences at 16 qubits:
  edge weights after k-NN sparsification   mean 0.0016
  node-feature MAD after propagation       0.003   (manuscript reports 0.45)


MECHANISM
---------
Measure concentration. The parcellation yields 200 regional states; a
65,536-dimensional Hilbert space has vastly more room than 200 states can
populate, so they spread out and every pairwise overlap approaches zero. This
is the well-known concentration-of-measure problem in quantum kernels, and it
is the same effect that produces barren plateaus in variational circuits
[Cerezo 2021, McClean 2018, Thanasilp 2024].


WHERE THIS CONTRADICTS THE MANUSCRIPT
-------------------------------------
Section III-C of the manuscript treats near-orthogonality as a benefit:

  "in high-dimensional Hilbert spaces, unrelated vectors tend to be close to
   orthogonal ... Low-relevance noise connections therefore land near zero
   fidelity by default, while genuine structural relationships stand out,
   giving a naturally sparser and cleaner adjacency matrix."

The measurement shows the mechanism has no operating window at 16 qubits.
Orthogonality is selective only while *some* pairs remain non-orthogonal. At
16 qubits everything is orthogonal, so nothing stands out — the claimed
average degree of k ~ 25 with visible DMN/ECN block structure cannot arise.

The correct statement is narrower and still useful: fidelity-based connectivity
requires a *narrow* register. The original notebook accompanying this work used
4 qubits, which the table above shows is a viable operating point. The scaling
to 16 qubits, motivated by "one qubit per PCA feature", silently destroyed the
metric the method depends on.


IMPLICATION FOR THE PAPER
-------------------------
This is publishable in its own right, as a design constraint with a measured
curve. It converts a vague appeal to "exponentially large Hilbert spaces" into
a quantitative statement: for N regions, the register must be narrow enough
that 2^n does not vastly exceed N, or the fidelity metric degenerates.

Recommended framing: report the curve, state the operating window, and cite the
concentration literature. Do not claim high-dimensional Hilbert space as an
unqualified advantage.

REPRODUCE
---------
  python - <<'PY'
  import torch
  from qagta.data.abide import load_abide
  from qagta.quantum.fmri_encoder import RingEntangledEncoder
  from qagta.quantum.fidelity import pairwise_fidelity
  for nq in (4, 8, 12, 16):
      ds = load_abide(limit=6, n_components=nq)
      enc = RingEntangledEncoder(nq)
      x = torch.as_tensor(ds.subjects[0].features)
      with torch.no_grad():
          z, s = enc(x, return_state=True)
          f = pairwise_fidelity(s); off = f[~torch.eye(200, dtype=bool)]
      print(nq, float(off.mean()), float(off.max()), float((off > 0.01).float().mean()))
  PY
