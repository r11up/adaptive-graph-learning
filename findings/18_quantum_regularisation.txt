FINDING 18 — WHERE QUANTUM MEASURABLY LEADS: RESISTANCE TO OVERFITTING
=======================================================================
Status: measured, all four cohorts, 62 folds, 4,456 subjects
Date:   2026-08-28
Code:   scripts/run_reupload.py (train accuracy recorded per fold)
Raw:    results/superseded-prefix/planC/{abide,adhd,mdd,ucla}/reupload_results.json


THE QUESTION
------------
Every matched comparison in this study returns a tie or a loss for the quantum
arm on accuracy. That is the headline, but "no advantage on accuracy" is not
the same as "no measurable difference." Recording training accuracy alongside
test accuracy makes a second quantity available -- the overfitting gap -- and
the quantum and classical arms do NOT behave the same on it.


RESULT
------
Overfitting gap (train accuracy - test accuracy), quantum arms averaged over
Q-Plain, Q-Reup1 and Q-Reup2:

  cohort            n      Q gap   C-CNN   C-MLP   C-Linear   Q vs C-MLP
  --------------  -----    -----   -----   -----   --------   -----------------
  REST-meta-MDD    2428    0.059   0.048   0.081     0.044    -0.022  p = 0.016
  ABIDE-I          1035    0.041   0.053   0.082     0.023    -0.041  p = 0.005
  ADHD-200          767    0.158   0.195   0.203     0.129    -0.045  p = 0.109
  UCLA-CNP          226    0.089   0.246   0.270     0.154    -0.181  p = 0.002

Paired Wilcoxon, fold by fold, pooled quantum gap against the C-MLP gap.
Quantum overfits less than the matched-capacity MLP on all four cohorts, and
significantly on three.


THE MECHANISM
-------------
A QCNN reads out a single Pauli-Z expectation bounded in [-1, 1], and the
affine layer that maps it to logits has two parameters. Whatever the circuit
computes, it must be squeezed through that bounded scalar. This caps what the
model can express and makes the circuit behave as a strong regulariser --
structurally, not by tuning.

That is a genuinely quantum-specific property in this comparison: it follows
from the measurement postulate, not from a hyperparameter.


IT SCALES WITH SCARCITY OF DATA
-------------------------------
Ranking the cohorts by size against how much less quantum overfits than C-MLP:

  n = 2428   advantage 0.022
  n = 1035   advantage 0.041
  n =  767   advantage 0.045
  n =  226   advantage 0.181

A perfect inverse ordering: the smaller the cohort, the larger the margin. At
n = 226 the C-MLP overfits three times as much as the quantum arms, and the
quantum arms have the SMALLEST gap of any model tested there -- beating even
the 18-parameter linear model (0.089 against 0.154).

STATISTICAL CARE: with four cohorts this ordering CANNOT be given a p < 0.05.
scipy's spearmanr returns rho = -1.000, p = 0.0000, but that is a t-based
approximation that is invalid at n = 4. The exact permutation null has 24
orderings, of which 2 reach |rho| = 1, so the exact two-sided p is 0.083.
Report the ordering descriptively. Do not quote scipy's p-value.


THE LIMIT — AND IT IS THE DECISIVE ONE
--------------------------------------
Less overfitting does not become more accuracy.

On the three larger cohorts the 18-parameter linear model has a SMALLER gap
than the quantum arms (0.023 vs 0.041 on ABIDE; 0.044 vs 0.059 on MDD; 0.129
vs 0.158 on ADHD) and equal or better accuracy, at roughly 1/100th the
runtime. Regularisation is easy to obtain classically; a bounded quantum
read-out is an expensive way to buy it.

UCLA-CNP is the one cohort where the quantum arms lead on both quantities --
smallest gap (0.089) and nominally best accuracy (Q-Plain 0.598 against
C-Linear 0.593). The accuracy difference is not significant, and n = 226 with
10 folds is the least-powered cohort in the study.

*** THIS WAS TESTED AND THE LOW-DATA READING IS FALSIFIED. See FINDING 21. ***
Controlled subsampling of ABIDE-I and REST-meta-MDD across seven training sizes
reproduces the REGULARISATION effect at every size (and strengthens it as n
falls, up to 2.2x) but produces NO accuracy crossover at any size. The UCLA-CNP
result reflects that cohort, not its sample size.


HOW TO STATE THIS
-----------------
Defensible: "Quantum circuits act as structural regularisers. They overfit
significantly less than a matched-capacity MLP on three of four cohorts, and
the margin grows as data becomes scarce. This does not translate into a
significant accuracy advantage, because a linear model regularises as well or
better at a hundredth of the cost."

NOT defensible: "Quantum machine learning offers an advantage in the low-data
regime." The accuracy comparison that would support that is non-significant on
every cohort, and the one cohort where quantum leads on both quantities is the
smallest and least-powered.


WHY IT MATTERS ANYWAY
---------------------
It is the only quantity in the study on which the quantum arm measurably and
reproducibly leads, and it identifies the regime where a quantum advantage, if
one exists for this problem, would be found first: small cohorts where
classical models cannot be regularised well enough. That is a testable
direction rather than a general claim, and it is the honest answer to a
reviewer asking under what conditions quantum would be preferred.
