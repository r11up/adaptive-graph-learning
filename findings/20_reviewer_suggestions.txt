1. DAGMNet "91.5% on ABIDE-I LOSO" — THE PROTOCOL IS WRONG
-----------------------------------------------------------
Verified against the paper (PMC12467520). DAGMNet is real and reports 91.59%
accuracy / 96.80% AUC on ABIDE-I. But:

  - it uses 10-FOLD CROSS-VALIDATION, not leave-one-site-out;
  - it is MULTIMODAL: sMRI + fMRI + phenotypic;
  - the phenotypic inputs include STUDY LOCATION, gender, age and IQ, used to
    build the population graph;
  - 871 subjects, three atlases (Harvard-Oxford, EZ, AAL), not CC200.

Site identity is therefore an input feature under random 10-fold splits, so
subjects from a site appear in both train and test. That is exactly the leakage
leave-site-out exists to prevent, and exactly the artefact mechanism our own
Section on apparent advantage documents.

DAGMNet is not a comparable benchmark for a unimodal, connectivity-only,
site-held-out model. It IS an excellent worked example for the artefact
section, and should be cited there rather than in the results comparison.

Related precedent already in the paper: the ADHD-200 competition's best imaging
entry (0.615) was beaten by phenotypic data ALONE (0.625).


2. HIERARCHICAL / ATTENTION POOLING — ALREADY TESTED, DOES NOT HELP
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The suggestion to replace global mean pooling with attention or Top-K pooling
is already answered by our read-out ablation (Table tab:readout):

  read-out            F1      accuracy   specificity
  ----------------   -----    --------   -----------
  Mean pooling       0.292     0.495       0.550
  Mean/max/std       0.295     0.500       0.554
  Attention pooling  0.133     0.512       0.800
  Ordered flatten    0.192     0.497       0.700

Every read-out sits at chance. Attention pooling gives the highest accuracy
(0.512) and the WORST F1 (0.133) -- it collapses toward a single class, which
the high specificity (0.800) confirms. Preserving regional identity is not the
binding constraint.

This matters for how the diagnosis is stated: the read-out was a reasonable
hypothesis, we tested four of them, and none moved accuracy off chance.


3. CONNECTIVITY-PROFILE NODE FEATURES — GENUINELY UNTESTED, AND INDICATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This one is right, and it is the single most promising untested change.

FINDING 06 established that per-region temporal PCA components carry no
diagnostic signal. The graph path still uses them as node features. Meanwhile
the SQK / model-suite path uses CONNECTIVITY features (correlation rows) and
reaches 0.649-0.671 on the same cohort and folds.

So the graph has never been given the features that demonstrably carry the
signal. Replacing PCA node features with compressed connectivity profiles is
directly indicated by our own diagnosis and has not been run. -> Experiment 3.


4. QUANTUM TIME-SERIES TRANSFORMER — REAL, RELEVANT, NOT REPLICABLE HERE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Verified: "Resting-state fMRI Analysis using Quantum Time-series Transformer"
(arXiv:2509.00711), also IEEE Xplore 11250368. Uses LCU and QSVT for attention
at polylogarithmic complexity, evaluated on ABCD and UK Biobank, reports
"especially pronounced gains in small-sample scenarios" and SHAP-identified
ADHD biomarkers. Built on Quixer (arXiv:2406.04305, Quantinuum).

Directly relevant, and worth citing prominently: it targets the same constraint
we measured (static pairwise fidelity concentrating) with a different mechanism.

NOTE THE TENSION WITH OUR OWN RESULT: QTT claims gains in small-sample
scenarios. Our low-data experiment (FINDING 21) found no such crossover for a
QCNN across seven training sizes on two cohorts. Different architecture, same
claimed regime -- worth stating as an open question rather than a contradiction.

Implementing LCU + QSVT attention faithfully is weeks of work and out of scope
for this paper. Cite and discuss; do not claim to have evaluated it.
