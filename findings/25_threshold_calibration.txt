FINDING 25 — THRESHOLD CALIBRATION DOES NOT CONVERT RANKING INTO ACCURACY
=========================================================================
Status: measured, all four cohorts, hypothesis FALSIFIED
Date:   2026-08-29
Code:   scripts/run_calibration.py
Raw:    results/calibration/{abide,adhd,mdd,ucla}/calibration_results.json


THE HYPOTHESIS
--------------
FINDING 24 measured an asymmetry: the quantum arm leads on AUC in 34 of 68
comparisons but on accuracy in only 24 of 72. AUC scores ranking; accuracy
scores ranking AND threshold placement. A QCNN maps a bounded Pauli-Z
expectation to logits through a two-parameter affine layer, a weaker
calibration mechanism than a classical network's final linear layer, so the
quantum arm might rank well and threshold badly.

That predicted a specific, cheap intervention: choose the decision threshold
per fold instead of defaulting to 0.5, and the quantum arm's ranking should
convert into accuracy.

An external review independently proposed the same experiment, which is a
reason to run it rather than to assume it.


DESIGN
------
Threshold chosen on TRAINING scores only, maximising Youden's J, then applied
unchanged to test scores. No test information reaches the choice.

BOTH arms calibrated identically. Calibrating only the quantum side would
replace a matched comparison with an unmatched one, which is the failure mode
FINDING 20 documents. The question is not who wins after calibration but who
GAINS more from it.


RESULT — NO ARM GAINS
---------------------
  cohort           Quantum   C-CNN   C-MLP   C-Linear
  --------------   -------   -----   -----   --------
  ABIDE-I           -0.001  -0.011  +0.005    -0.006
  ADHD-200          +0.013  +0.014  +0.019    +0.000
  REST-meta-MDD     -0.005  +0.017  -0.009    -0.013
  UCLA-CNP          -0.009  +0.017  -0.009    -0.018
  --------------   -------   -----   -----   --------
  mean              -0.000  +0.009  +0.002    -0.009

The quantum arm's mean gain across four cohorts is ZERO to three decimals. Of
sixteen model-cohort pairs only one reaches p < 0.05, and it goes the wrong
way: C-Linear on REST-meta-MDD LOSES 0.013 accuracy (p = 0.028) when its
threshold is tuned.

ABIDE-I is the decisive case and the cleanest refutation. It is where the
quantum arm has its highest AUC (0.679) and therefore the most ranking quality
available to convert. Calibration there yields -0.001.

THE THRESHOLD-PLACEMENT EXPLANATION IS WRONG.


WHAT THIS CORRECTS IN FINDING 24
--------------------------------
Running this also exposed an over-broad claim of my own. FINDING 24 framed the
result as "quantum ranks as well or better than matched classical models". The
matched-pair statement is correct -- QCNN beats its matched CNN by +0.037 AUC
(p = 0.033) on ABIDE-I. The general one is not.

Against the BEST classical arm in the same run:

  cohort           Quantum   best classical      verdict
  --------------   -------   ---------------     -------
  ABIDE-I            0.679   0.679  (C-Linear)   ties
  ADHD-200           0.548   0.563  (C-Linear)   behind
  REST-meta-MDD      0.566   0.582  (C-CNN)      behind
  UCLA-CNP           0.590   0.603  (C-Linear)   behind

Quantum ties the best classical arm on ONE cohort and is behind on three. The
AUC advantage is specifically over the convolutional comparator, not over
classical models generally. FINDING 24 and the paper both need this narrowing.

This is the same discipline applied earlier to C-CNN, now applied to a claim
that favoured us.


WHY THE ASYMMETRY EXISTS, IF NOT THRESHOLDS
-------------------------------------------
Unresolved, and worth stating as open. Two candidates the data does not
separate:

1. The AUC/accuracy gap is a property of WHICH comparator, not of calibration.
   C-CNN ranks poorly and thresholds poorly; quantum beats it on both. Against
   C-Linear, quantum ties on AUC and on accuracy. On that reading there is no
   asymmetry to explain -- only a weak comparator.

2. Youden's J on training scores is itself a poor estimator at these sample
   sizes and fold counts, so the calibration is real but too noisy to help.
   Nested cross-validation for the threshold would test this and was not run.

Candidate 1 is the simpler explanation and is consistent with everything else
in the study.


WHAT TO REPORT
--------------
The reviewer's recommendation was sound and worth acting on; the result is
that it does not work. Report the experiment and the null, because "adjust the
threshold" is the obvious first response to an AUC/accuracy gap and the field
benefits from knowing it was tried and measured here.
