FINDING 11 — MATCHED COMPARISON: QUANTUM IS EQUIVALENT, NOT WORSE, AND NOT BETTER
=================================================================================
Status: measured, four cohorts, 62 folds, supervised feature pipeline
Date:   2026-08-28
Raw:    results/{ABIDE,ADHD200,MDD,UCLA}_benchmark


THE PIPELINE CHANGE THAT MATTERED
---------------------------------
Earlier studies (FINDINGS 08, 10) reduced the connectivity vector with
UNSUPERVISED PCA. PCA maximises variance, not class separation, so it can
discard the directions that distinguish groups. Replacing it with SUPERVISED
selection — top-k connections by absolute t-statistic, fit on the training
sites only — lifted ABIDE held-out AUC from 0.729 to 0.738 and, more
importantly, changed the quantum comparison from "significantly worse" to
"statistically indistinguishable".

Selection is fit inside each training fold. Applying it to the full cohort
would leak held-out labels into feature selection, which is a common and
serious error in this literature.

THE CENTRAL RESULT: EQUIVALENCE UNDER MATCHED FEATURES
------------------------------------------------------
"Matched" means the classical RBF sees exactly the n_qubits features the
quantum kernel sees. Without that control a quantum loss cannot be attributed:
the register caps the quantum kernel at 8 features while full models get 2000,
so any gap would measure dimensionality rather than the kernel.

  cohort          quantum vs matched classical
  -------------   -----------------------------
  ABIDE-I          9/20 wins, p = 0.510
  ADHD-200         2/7  wins, p = 0.562
  REST-meta-MDD   10/25 wins, p = 0.590
  UCLA-CNP         4/10 wins, p = 0.250

No cohort shows a significant difference in either direction, across 62 folds.
The quantum fidelity kernel is statistically indistinguishable from a classical
RBF kernel on the same inputs.

This is a more precise and more defensible statement than the earlier
"quantum is worse" (FINDING 10). That conclusion was an artefact of comparing
8 quantum features against 128 classical ones. With the comparison properly
matched, the quantum kernel neither gains nor loses.

Where quantum does lose — against the full 2000-feature models on ABIDE and
MDD — the cause is the register limit, not the kernel. FINDING 01 explains why
the register cannot simply be widened.


POSITION AGAINST PUBLISHED RESULTS
----------------------------------
Reported under comparable leave-one-site-out protocols:

  cohort          published                                    ours
  -------------   ------------------------------------------   ---------
  ABIDE-I         0.51-0.56 traditional ML (LOSO)               0.649
                  ~0.67 Abraham et al. 2017
                  0.82 multimodal cross-attention (2026)
  ADHD-200        0.615 competition best, imaging               0.571
                  0.625 phenotypic data alone
                  0.637 social-network method
  REST-meta-MDD   0.73 ensemble GNN (LOSO)                      0.603
                  0.776 MMDD multimodal

Our pipeline is mid-range: competitive on ABIDE, below the best published on
ADHD-200 and MDD. It is a deliberately simple pipeline — supervised selection,
a standard classifier, no multimodal fusion, no learned harmonisation, no
architecture search — and it is not tuned to win a leaderboard. It exists to
give the quantum comparison a well-tuned classical baseline rather than a weak
one, which it now does.

Reported ABIDE figures above ~0.85 almost always use random splits rather than
site-held-out splits and are not comparable to any number in this table.
