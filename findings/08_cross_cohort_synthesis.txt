FINDING 08 — NO QUANTUM ADVANTAGE ACROSS FOUR COHORTS; THE ONE POSITIVE REVERSES
================================================================================
Status: measured, four cohorts, the central claim of the study
Date:   2026-08-28
Raw:    results/MDD_qkernel, results/UCLA_qkernel_cv10, results/adhd200_qkernel


THE COMPARISON
--------------
Subject-level quantum kernel SVM against classical kernels on identical
descriptors and identical folds. 8 qubits, 8 PCA components, bandwidth and
kernel type searched within each fold by kernel-target alignment, classical
side additionally sweeping gamma.

  REST-meta-MDD    2428  25     0.571       0.549   0.623      0.671
  ADHD-200          767   7     0.354       0.336   0.310      0.237
  UCLA-CNP          226  10     0.615       0.426   0.524      0.622

  paired Wilcoxon, quantum vs classical:
  cohort           vs RBF                    vs linear
  --------------   -----------------------   -----------------------
  REST-meta-MDD    14/25 wins, p = 0.771     7/25 wins, p = 0.0105 *
  ADHD-200          3/7  wins, p = 0.938     4/7  wins, p = 0.578
  UCLA-CNP          4/10 wins, p = 0.557     8/10 wins, p = 0.0371 *

  * significant at 0.05 uncorrected, in OPPOSITE directions


THE HEADLINE: THE POSITIVE RESULT DOES NOT REPLICATE
----------------------------------------------------
UCLA-CNP produced the study's only quantum win: the kernel beat the classical
linear kernel on 8 of 10 folds, p = 0.0371.

On REST-meta-MDD the same comparison runs the other way and is also
significant: the linear kernel beats the quantum kernel on 18 of 25 folds,
p = 0.0105.

MDD is the better-powered test by a wide margin — 2428 subjects against 226,
25 folds against 10 — so where the two disagree, MDD should be believed. The
UCLA result is most economically explained as noise surfaced by running
several comparisons: six paired tests across three cohorts, of which two
reached p < 0.05 in opposite directions, is close to what multiple testing
alone predicts. Neither survives Bonferroni correction (0.05/6 = 0.0083).

Reporting the UCLA number alone would be the single most misleading thing this
study could do.


SUPPORTING EVIDENCE THAT THE WIN WAS NOT REAL
---------------------------------------------
1. AUC is at chance on UCLA for every model (0.490-0.514). An F1 gap with
   chance-level AUC reflects where the decision threshold happened to fall,
   not better ranking of subjects. On MDD, where AUC does separate
   (0.544-0.684), the quantum kernel is not best.

2. The win was against the LINEAR kernel only, never against RBF, on any
   cohort. "A non-linear kernel beats a linear one" is a statement about the
   data, not about quantum computing.

3. Effect direction is inconsistent across cohorts, which is the signature of
   noise rather than a mechanism.


THE DIMENSIONALITY RESULT IS LARGER THAN ANY QUANTUM EFFECT
------------------------------------------------------------
On MDD the full-dimensional classical RBF reaches F1 0.671 and AUC 0.684,
against 0.571 / 0.544 for the quantum kernel on 8 PCA components. The cost of
compressing 19,900 correlations to 8 components dwarfs every quantum-versus-
classical difference measured here.

This is the binding constraint. Any quantum method restricted to a narrow
register inherits it, and FINDING 01 shows why the register cannot simply be
widened: fidelity concentrates to zero. The two constraints act against each
other — narrow registers preserve the kernel but destroy the features, wide
registers preserve the features but destroy the kernel.


STATUS OF THE CENTRAL CLAIM
---------------------------
Across four cohorts (ABIDE I, ADHD-200, REST-meta-MDD, UCLA-CNP), two framings
(region-level graph topology, subject-level kernel), and every lever tested
(register width, bandwidth, kernel type, selection rule, descriptor,
read-out), no configuration demonstrates a reproducible advantage for quantum
methods over matched classical baselines.

This is a negative result, and it is worth publishing as one. It is measured
on four public cohorts under site-held-out or stratified validation, with
paired tests, matched features and matched tuning budgets — conditions under
which much of the published quantum-neuroimaging literature does not report.
