PARCELLATION MISMATCH WITH ABIDE
--------------------------------
Every ADHD-200 Athena file has 190 ROIs; every ABIDE PCP file has 200. The
CC200 atlas specifies 200 clusters but the normalised-cut procedure resolves
to 190, and the two releases made different choices about which to ship.

Consequence: the cohorts CANNOT be pooled into one graph model, because node
counts differ. Each must be reported as its own Leave-Site-Out experiment.
This is a further argument for the per-cohort reporting already recommended on
preprocessing-confound grounds.

A handful of subjects also carry duplicated ROI columns, making the
correlation matrix singular and causing LAPACK's divide-and-conquer SVD to
fail outright. Handled with a fallback to the gesvd driver and, failing that,
a small diagonal jitter, so affected subjects stay in the cohort rather than
being silently dropped.


QUANTUM KERNEL RESULT
---------------------
8 qubits, matched 8-component descriptors, identical folds, alignment-based
selection, bandwidth and kernel type searched within each fold:

  model                        F1               accuracy         AUC
  --------------------------   --------------   --------------   --------------
  quantum kernel               0.354 +- 0.037   0.536 +- 0.027   0.528 +- 0.036
  classical RBF                0.336 +- 0.142   0.523 +- 0.062   0.552 +- 0.034
  classical linear             0.310 +- 0.141   0.568 +- 0.070   0.526 +- 0.066
  reference: RBF full-dim      0.237 +- 0.155   0.574 +- 0.096   0.554 +- 0.075

  quantum vs RBF:     median dF1 -0.053, wins 3/7, Wilcoxon p = 0.94
  quantum vs linear:  median dF1 +0.067, wins 4/7, Wilcoxon p = 0.58

The quantum kernel posts the highest mean F1 and the narrowest interval, but
neither paired test approaches significance and its AUC is not the best. There
is no quantum advantage here. Reporting the F1 ranking alone would be
misleading, which is why all three metrics and both paired tests are given.

Note also that every model sits near chance (AUC 0.52-0.55). ADHD-200 is a
genuinely hard benchmark: the original competition's winning entry reached
about 61% accuracy, and much of the >90% literature uses random splits rather
than site-held-out validation. These numbers are consistent with the honest
ceiling for this cohort under LSO, not evidence of a broken pipeline.


STATUS OF THE CENTRAL CLAIM
---------------------------
Across two cohorts (ABIDE I, ADHD-200) and two framings (region-level graph
topology, subject-level kernel), no configuration has demonstrated a
statistically significant advantage for quantum methods over matched classical
baselines under Leave-Site-Out validation.
