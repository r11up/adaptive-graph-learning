FINDING 15 — PLAN B (BLOCK ENSEMBLE): HELPS CLASSICAL, NOT QUANTUM
===================================================================
Status: measured, ABIDE-I, 20 folds, full cohort
Date:   2026-08-28
Raw:    results/ABIDE_ensemble/ensemble_results.json


THE IDEA
--------
The register caps one circuit at 8 features and FINDING 01 shows it cannot be
widened. Plan B sidesteps the cap: split the top 32 features into 4 disjoint
blocks, train one narrow model per block, combine with learned weights. The
ensemble sees 32 features while every circuit stays at 8.

Two comparators, controlling different things:
  C-Ensemble  same partition, same combiner, classical members -> isolates model
  C-Single    one classical model given all 32 features at once -> bounds what
              the block partition costs


RESULT (ABIDE-I, 20 folds)
--------------------------
  model         accuracy   F1      AUC     sec/fold
  -----------   --------   -----   -----   --------
  Q-Ensemble     0.592     0.570   0.641    30.6
  C-Ensemble     0.622     0.602   0.677     0.2
  C-Single       0.603     0.590   0.658     0.1

  Q-Ensemble vs C-Ensemble:  -0.028  wins 5/20  p = 0.0475 *
  Q-Ensemble vs C-Single:    -0.015  wins 8/20  p = 0.5869

  mean learned block weight: 0.326  0.207  0.257  0.211


WHAT IT SHOWS
-------------
Ensembling helps the classical arm: C-Ensemble 0.622 against C-Single 0.603,
so partitioning into blocks and combining is worth about +0.019 accuracy over
one model on the same 32 features.

The quantum arm does not get that benefit. Q-Ensemble at 0.592 loses to
C-Ensemble significantly (p = 0.0475) and is indistinguishable from C-Single.
It is also no better than a single QCNN on 8 features (0.610 in FINDING 13,
though under a different epoch and learning-rate budget, so not a strict
comparison).

Learned block weights are near-even, so the combiner is not collapsing onto one
block — every block contributes and the ensemble is genuinely using its 32
features.


THE SUBSET WAS MISLEADING, AGAIN
--------------------------------
On 300 subjects and 6 folds, Q-Ensemble beat C-Single by +0.041 (4/6 wins),
which looked like the block construction was buying the quantum arm something.
On the full cohort that is -0.015 at p = 0.59 — no effect.

This is the second time in two Plans that a six-fold subset pointed the wrong
way (FINDING 14 records the first). Subsets of this size are not adequate for
judging these differences, and no intermediate result from one should be
reported.


CUMULATIVE POSITION
-------------------
Plan A (learned projection): classical +0.042, quantum -0.061, all four paired
tests significant against quantum.
Plan B (block ensemble): classical +0.019 over its own single-model control,
quantum flat, significantly behind the matched classical ensemble.

Both plans targeted the feature budget that FINDING 11 identified, and both
confirm the diagnosis was right about the headroom while showing the quantum
head cannot convert it. Across four architectural families now — kernel,
population graph, learned projection, block ensemble — every intervention that
helps the quantum arm helps the matched classical arm at least as much.
