FINDING 19 — THE VARIATIONAL LAYER IS INVISIBLE TO THE FIDELITY GRAPH
======================================================================
Status: measured, algebraic identity confirmed numerically to float32 precision
Date:   2026-08-29
Code:   src/qagta/quantum/fmri_encoder.py
Origin: raised in an external methodology review (To Do List Docs, 2026-08-27);
        the claim did not reproduce on first attempt because of an error in our
        own test, then reproduced exactly once the test was corrected.


THE CLAIM
---------
A unitary applied identically to both states in a fidelity computation cancels:

    F_ij = |<psi_i|psi_j>|^2
         = |<0| V(x_i)^dag U^dag U V(x_j) |0>|^2
         = |<0| V(x_i)^dag V(x_j) |0>|^2          since U^dag U = I

So everything after the data-dependent encoding V(x) leaves the fidelity kernel
exactly invariant, and receives exactly zero gradient from the graph.


VERIFICATION
------------
Our encoder is  |psi(x)> = RX(phi_x) RZ(phi_z) . CNOT_ring . RY(encode_scale * x) |0>
with phi_z, phi_x, encode_scale shared across all 200 regions.

  parameter        grad from fidelity     grad from <Z> read-out
  --------------   -------------------    ----------------------
  encode_scale        5.149e+01              1.227e+01
  phi_z               1.013e-07              1.281e-01
  phi_x               3.795e-08              2.978e-01

Perturbing phi_z by +1.3 and phi_x by -0.7 changes the fidelity Gram matrix by
max |dF| = 1.19e-06 -- float32 roundoff.

CONFIRMED. phi_z and phi_x are algebraically invisible to the graph.
encode_scale is not, because it sits INSIDE V(x).

A NOTE ON HOW THIS WAS VERIFIED: the first test returned non-zero gradients
(5.9e-02, 1.9e-01) and appeared to refute the claim. That test was wrong --
forward() returns Z-expectations unless return_state=True, so it computed an
"overlap" between real expectation vectors rather than statevectors. External
claims should be checked, but so should the check.


WHAT IT MEANS
-------------
The graph-construction stage is trainable through exactly ONE parameter vector:
encode_scale, of length n_qubits. Not through the entangling ring, and not
through the variational layer. At 8 qubits that is 8 trainable parameters
governing a 200-node topology, against the ~24 in the full encoder and far more
in the manuscript's description of an end-to-end trainable measurement process.

This does NOT invalidate the empirical results. The pipeline still trains, the
graph still adapts, and every reported number stands -- the classifier and the
GAT are unaffected, and the <Z> read-out path (used by QCNN, VQC and the whole
model suite) has full gradient flow. What changes is the mechanism: the
"adaptive" graph has an order of magnitude less capacity than the architecture
implies.

It is also a third, independent reason the graph pipeline sits at chance,
alongside fidelity collapse (FINDING 01) and the read-out bottleneck
(FINDING 06). Those are about information reaching the graph and information
leaving it; this one is about how little of the graph can be learned at all.


THE TRANSFERABLE RULE
---------------------
For any fidelity-based (state-overlap) graph construction:

    Trainable parameters must sit INSIDE the data-dependent encoding.
    Anything applied after it -- ansatz, entangler, however deep -- cancels.

This is a design constraint on the whole family of quantum-fidelity graph
methods, not a property of our implementation. Published architectures that
place a variational ansatz after the feature map and describe the resulting
fidelity graph as "trainable" are training only whatever lies inside the map.


WHY IT STRENGTHENS THE PAPER
----------------------------
The honest version of the architecture claim is narrower and more defensible:
the fidelity graph is trainable through the encoding gains, and the deeper
variational structure serves the read-out path rather than the graph. Stating
this pre-empts exactly the objection an informed reviewer would raise, and it
converts a latent correctness problem into a design rule other groups can use.
