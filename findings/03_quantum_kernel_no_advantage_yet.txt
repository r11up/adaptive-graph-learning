FINDING 03 — QUANTUM KERNEL: NO ADVANTAGE YET, AND A NEAR-MISS FALSE POSITIVE
==============================================================================
Status: measured; negative so far; includes a corrected methodological error
Date:   2026-08-15
Code:   src/qagta/quantum/kernel.py, scripts/run_quantum_kernel.py
Data:   ABIDE I, Leave-Site-Out, subject-level correlation descriptors


MOTIVATION
----------
FINDING 01 shows fidelity between *regions* degenerates at 16 qubits. But the
same primitive can be applied between *subjects*, giving a kernel matrix

    K_ij = |<psi(x_i)|psi(x_j)>|^2

for a precomputed-kernel SVM. This is the Havlicek/Schuld quantum kernel
setting [1,2]. It needs only 4-8 qubits for a compressed subject descriptor,
which is inside the operating window FINDING 01 identifies, and it competes
with the classical SVM using the same classifier and the same folds — changing
only the kernel. Published work reports a 4-qubit quantum kernel SVM reaching
AUC 0.957 on UCLA CNP for ADHD vs control [3], so the approach is credible.


THE FALSE POSITIVE, AND WHY IT MATTERS
--------------------------------------
The first sweep appeared to show a decisive quantum win:

    quantum vs classical RBF: median dF1 = +0.489, wins 20/20, Wilcoxon p = 0.0001

This was NOT a quantum advantage. The classical RBF baseline had collapsed:

    classical RBF   F1 = 0.000 +- 0.000   (all-negative prediction, every fold)

Cause: hyperparameters were selected by F1 on the TRAINING set. With an RBF
kernel, high gamma fits the training split perfectly (train F1 = 1.0), so the
grid search reliably chose the most overfit model, which then predicted a
single class on the held-out site. The quantum kernel, being flatter, was less
able to overfit and so was less damaged by the same bad selection rule.

The asymmetry is what exposed it: quantum beat RBF 20/20 while LOSING to
classical linear. A method that genuinely separates classes does not beat one
classical kernel unanimously and lose to a simpler one.

Fix: select hyperparameters by inner 3-fold stratified cross-validation on the
training sites, scored by AUC, for both quantum and classical arms.

Lesson worth stating in any writeup: a baseline scoring exactly 0.000 with zero
variance is never a result, it is a bug. Quantum-advantage claims are especially
vulnerable to this failure mode, because a weak quantum model can "win" simply
by being harder to overfit than a mis-tuned classical comparator.


CORRECTED RESULT
----------------
8 qubits / 8 PCA components, 400-subject subset, identical folds:

  model                        F1               accuracy         AUC
  --------------------------   --------------   --------------   --------------
  quantum kernel               0.560 +- 0.166   0.529 +- 0.068   0.592 +- 0.048
  classical RBF                0.548 +- 0.103   0.587 +- 0.046   0.633 +- 0.077
  classical linear             0.546 +- 0.137   0.588 +- 0.063   0.635 +- 0.073
  reference: RBF on full dim   0.617 +- 0.079   0.633 +- 0.036   0.682 +- 0.073

  quantum vs classical RBF:    median dF1 = +0.021, wins 5/8, p = 0.945
  quantum vs classical linear: median dF1 = -0.034, wins 3/8, p = 0.945

Conclusion: at parity on F1, slightly behind on AUC, not significant either way.
NO QUANTUM ADVANTAGE IS DEMONSTRATED at this configuration.


WHAT REMAINS UNTESTED
---------------------
The search space has barely been explored. Untried, in priority order:

1. Feature-map design. The current ZZ-style map is the textbook default. The
   bandwidth of the encoding (angle scaling) is known to control the
   concentration/expressivity trade-off and is the single most influential
   hyperparameter in quantum kernels [4]. It has not been tuned at all here.
2. Descriptor choice. PCA on correlation vectors is a generic reduction that
   discards the network structure the quantum map might exploit. Graph-derived
   or connectivity-motif descriptors may suit the map better.
3. Qubit/repetition sweep with the corrected selection. The earlier sweep is
   invalid and was discarded.
4. Kernel-target alignment as a selection criterion instead of accuracy.

An honest reading: the reframe is sound and the machinery is now correct, but
the default configuration does not beat a tuned classical kernel. Reporting
otherwise would require exactly the kind of broken baseline this finding
documents.
