FINDING 13 — QISKIT QUANTUM MODEL SUITE ACROSS FOUR COHORTS
============================================================
Status: measured, 4 cohorts, 62 folds, 9 models per fold
Date:   2026-08-28
Raw:    results/20260828-042703Z_qmodels/


WHAT WAS RUN
------------
Five quantum models from the Qiskit Machine Learning tutorials, each against
the classical comparator it is naturally matched to, on identical features
(8 supervised-selected connections) and identical folds.

  QCNN          Quantum CNN, Qiskit reference circuits (Cong et al. 2019),
                validated gate-for-gate against Qiskit to 1e-6
  VQC           ZZFeatureMap + RealAmplitudes + parity read-out
  QSVM-fixed    Fidelity kernel SVM, bandwidth by alignment
  QSVM-Pegasos  Qiskit PegasosQSVC on the same precomputed kernel
  TQEK          Trainable quantum embedding kernel (alignment-optimised)

  matched against: CNN (1-D), MLP, SVM-RBF, SVM-RBF, Trainable-RBF

THE RESULT
----------
Twenty paired comparisons. Three reach p < 0.05 uncorrected, and they do not
agree:

  TQEK beats its classical comparator on ABIDE (p = 0.049)
  QSVM-Pegasos LOSES on ABIDE (p = 0.039)
  VQC LOSES on MDD (p = 0.040)

With 20 tests, one expects roughly one false positive at alpha = 0.05 by
chance; three, in inconsistent directions, is consistent with noise. None
survives Bonferroni correction (0.05/20 = 0.0025). The largest observed
effect in either direction is 0.11 accuracy, on the smallest cohort.

The TQEK result is the study's best case for a quantum advantage and it does
not hold up: +0.028 on ABIDE (p = 0.049, right at threshold), but -0.013 on
MDD and -0.045 on UCLA. The same pattern as FINDINGS 08 and 10 — a positive
result on one cohort contradicted by others, with the larger cohorts
disagreeing.

QCNN is the most consistent quantum model, positive on three of four cohorts
(+0.020, +0.035, +0.044) and flat on the fourth, but no comparison approaches
significance and the effects are small.


TWO OBSERVATIONS WORTH RECORDING
--------------------------------
1. QSVM-Pegasos reports no AUC. PegasosQSVC exposes no decision function, so
   only its thresholded predictions are available. On ADHD-200 it returns
   F1 = 0.000 — it predicts a single class on every fold there. Its accuracy of
   0.561 on that cohort is therefore the majority-class rate, not a result.
   Reporting Pegasos accuracy without checking F1 would have been misleading.

2. Runtime differs by three orders of magnitude and favours the classical side
   in every case: QCNN 31 s/fold on MDD against 24 s for the classical CNN, but
   QSVM-fixed 0.6 s against SVM-RBF at 0.1 s, and TQEK 3.5 s against 0.1 s.
   Since accuracy is statistically indistinguishable, the classical models
   dominate on cost.


STATUS OF THE CENTRAL CLAIM
---------------------------
Five quantum model families, four cohorts, 62 folds, matched features, matched
folds, matched training budgets, paired testing. No quantum model shows a
reproducible advantage over its classical counterpart.

Combined with FINDINGS 08, 10 and 11, that is now four independent experimental
families reaching the same conclusion on the same data.
